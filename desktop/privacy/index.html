Privacy Policy

Desktop for iOS · Effective 13 October 2026

Desktop is a place to keep the things you save. Your library lives on your device. There is no account to create, and nothing you save is tied to your identity. If you turn on sync, an optional Premium feature, your library is encrypted on your device before it leaves, so the copy we store is one we cannot read.

This policy explains exactly what stays on your phone, the few things that leave it and why, and who else is involved when you view something you saved. It describes the app as it actually works, not a category of app in general.

Desktop is published by VRKIVE LLC. "We" and "us" below mean VRKIVE LLC.

No account, no identifiers

Desktop has no sign-up, sign-in, profile or cloud library until you turn on sync, and most people never will. It does not assign you a user ID. It does not read your device's advertising identifier or vendor identifier. It contains no analytics, attribution or crash-reporting service, and no third-party SDKs at all.

Desktop does not track you as the App Store defines tracking: nothing it sends is linked to you or your device, and nothing is shared with data brokers or advertisers. We do not sell or rent information about you.

What stays on your device

All of it, unless this policy says otherwise. Specifically:

  • Everything you save — links, images, videos — along with their titles, captions, creators and thumbnails.
  • Your folders, topics, ordering, wallpaper and appearance settings.
  • Whether you've finished onboarding, and your lifetime count of saved items (the number behind the free allowance).

Saved media is written to Desktop's own app container and to the App Group container it shares with its share extension. Unless you turn on sync, we hold no copy of your library. Videos you save stay here in every case and are never sent anywhere, sync or not. The one exception to this section is automatic topic sorting, which sends details of a saved post or link for classification; that is described in full below.

What leaves your device

Four things, in specific moments. None of them carries an identifier for you or your device. Sync and folder sharing, if you use them, are described in their own sections below.

1. Fetching details about a link you saved

When you save a link, Desktop needs its title, creator and cover image to show you a real card instead of a bare URL. The link's address is sent to our enrich service, which requests the publicly available details for it. For ordinary web pages, your device fetches the page preview itself, so that request goes to the site directly and not through us.

Our service keeps no record of the link. Its operational logs record only the site's hostname (for example "youtube.com"), the provider used and whether the lookup succeeded — never the full address.

2. Sorting a save onto the right Desktop

Desktop files each save under a topic for you. It does this by sending a short description of what you saved to our classify service, which passes it to a hosted AI model and gets back a single topic word.

Your own photos and videos are never sent. Anything you save from your camera roll or from a file stays on your device and is never transmitted for sorting. Those saves stay in Unsorted until you choose a topic for them yourself, which you can do from the viewer at any time.

What is sent is drawn from the post or page you saved — material that was already published at the address you saved it from:

If you saved…Desktop sends…
A post or link Its title, caption, creator name and handle, community (such as a subreddit) and host, plus the post's cover thumbnail where the platform provides one.
One of your own photos or videos Nothing. These are never sent.

It is used for nothing but choosing the topic word. We store none of it: the service holds it only for the moment it takes to answer, and its logs record only the source type, the model used and the outcome.

The request is passed to a hosted model provider (currently OpenAI; if that ever changes we will update this policy before it does). We have turned off retention on our side of that account, and they do not use it to train their models. They may retain it briefly for their own abuse monitoring before deleting it. Because that window is longer than the request itself, we disclose this as data collection on the App Store rather than treat it as purely transient.

No account, user ID or device identifier accompanies the request, and we attach nothing that would let it be joined to anything else you saved.

We would rather be precise than reassuring about what that does and does not mean. It means we do not label the request with who you are. It does not mean the content itself is anonymous: a title or caption can carry a name, and the page you saved may say something about you simply by being the page you saved. What it does mean is that the material in question was already public at the address you saved it from before Desktop ever touched it. That distinction is why your own photos and videos are excluded from this entirely rather than merely disclosed — a picture out of your library was never anywhere, and no wording in a policy would make sending it the same kind of act.

Sorting is not retroactive: a library that accumulated before this feature existed is not sent, and those saves stay in Unsorted until you file them.

You can set a topic yourself on any save, from the ⋯ menu in the viewer, and a topic you choose is permanent — automatic sorting never overrides it. Your saves also group by app independently of any of this, under Apps.

3. Checking the app's configuration

Desktop asks our config service for current settings, such as the size of the free allowance. This request sends no information about you or your library.

4. Viewing something you saved

When you open a saved post or page, Desktop loads it from its original source — Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit, YouTube, or the website itself. Those requests go from your device to that platform, not through us. As with opening the same link in a browser, the platform receives your IP address and any cookies your device already holds for it, and what they do with that is governed by their privacy policies rather than this one. Desktop adds no identifier of its own to these requests and cannot see their contents.

Sync (optional, Premium, end-to-end encrypted)

Sync keeps your library the same on every device you use. It is off until you open the Sync screen in the menu and sign in with Apple, and it is only offered to subscribers. Until you do that, nothing in this section applies to you.

Encrypted on your device. Before anything leaves your phone, Desktop encrypts it with a key that only your devices hold: your saved links with their titles, captions, creators and topics; your screenshots and photos saved from your own library; the frame used as a video's cover; your folders, their names and which saves are in them. We store that encrypted copy and cannot read it. Videos themselves are never uploaded and stay on the device that saved them.

What we can see. Encryption hides what you saved, not that you use sync. Our service can see an identifier for your account and for each record, whether a record is a save, a folder or a folder membership, when records change or are deleted, how large the encrypted files are, and when your devices sync.

The key. The key is made on your device the first time you sync and reaches your other devices only through iCloud Keychain. It is never sent to us. If every copy of it is lost, we cannot recover your synced library; the library on your phone is unaffected, and deleting your sync account lets you start a fresh copy from it.

Signing in. Desktop asks Apple for a sign-in only, not your name or email address. Apple gives our sign-in service a stable identifier for you, unique to Desktop, which we use only to tell your encrypted library apart from everyone else's.

Confirming the subscription. When you sign in, and from time to time after, the signed transaction Apple gives your device is sent to our service so it can confirm that the subscription is active before accepting your data. We keep the product, its expiry and Apple's transaction identifier for it; it carries no payment details.

Where it lives. On Supabase, in the United States, under an account we hold with them. It is reachable only through your sign-in and only while your subscription is active.

Deleting it. Tap Delete Account on the Sync screen and everything synced is removed at once. Sign Out stops sync but leaves what is already there. If your subscription ends, sync pauses and the data is deleted 90 days later unless you subscribe again. Deleting the app alone does not delete the synced copy; use Delete Account first.

Sharing a folder

You can share any folder as a link, whether or not you subscribe. Nothing is shared until you choose Share Folder and send the link yourself.

What is in it. The folder's name and emoji, and for each saved link: its address, title, caption, creator, the details Desktop shows on its card, and a small cover image. Photos, screenshots and videos from your own library are never included.

Encrypted on your device. The folder is encrypted on your phone with a new key for each link. The encrypted copy is uploaded to our share service; the key travels only inside the link, in the part after the #, which browsers and apps never send to a server. We store the encrypted copy and cannot read it. Anyone you give the link to, and anyone they pass it on to, can open the folder.

No account. Uploading a folder carries no account or device identifier. To limit abuse, the service counts uploads per network address for an hour, using a scrambled form of the address that changes every hour and is deleted after two.

Opening a link. In Desktop, the folder is downloaded and decrypted on the recipient's device. On the web, the page at desktopsaves.com decrypts it in the browser; that page is hosted by GitHub, which receives the visitor's network address as for any website.

How long it lasts. A link works for 30 days. After that our service stops serving it and deletes the encrypted copy at its next cleanup. Anyone who added the folder to their own Desktop keeps their copy.

Subscriptions and payment

Desktop's subscription is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the entire transaction. We never receive or have access to your payment method, card number or billing address. The app only asks iOS on your device whether a subscription is currently active; that answer never leaves your phone, except as part of turning on sync, described above.

Your count of saved items is also kept in your device's keychain, so that reinstalling the app doesn't hand out a fresh set of free saves. It is stored only on that device, is never synchronised to iCloud or to us, and is a number — it contains nothing about what you saved.

Permissions Desktop does not ask for

Desktop requests no access to your photo library, camera, microphone, location, contacts or notifications, and does not ask permission to track. Items arrive through the iOS share sheet: you choose each one, and Desktop receives only what you shared with it.

Deleting your data

  • Delete any saved item inside the app. Deleting it removes the item and its media — including any reduced copy kept for sorting — from your device.
  • Deleting a folder removes only the grouping. The saves inside it stay in your library; delete them individually to remove their content.
  • Delete Desktop from your phone to remove its library, media and settings entirely.

If you never turned on sync, we hold no library and no account, so there is nothing for us to delete on your behalf and no account deletion request to make. If you did, Delete Account on the Sync screen removes everything synced immediately; no request to us is needed. A shared folder's encrypted copy is deleted after its link expires at 30 days. The only value that deliberately outlives an uninstall is the lifetime save count described above.

Children

Desktop is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13. Desktop can display the pages and posts you save, which come from the open web, so its App Store age rating reflects that its content is not filtered by us.
