Privacy Policy

Desktop for iOS · Effective 29 September 2026

Desktop is a place to keep the things you save. Your library lives on your device. There is no account to create, and nothing you save is ever tied to your identity — because Desktop never learns it.

This policy explains exactly what stays on your phone, the few things that leave it and why, and who else is involved when you view something you saved. It describes the app as it actually works, not a category of app in general.

Desktop is published by VRKIVE LLC. "We" and "us" below mean VRKIVE LLC.

No account, no identifiers

Desktop has no sign-up, sign-in, profile or cloud library. It does not assign you a user ID. It does not read your device's advertising identifier or vendor identifier. It contains no analytics, attribution or crash-reporting service, and no third-party SDKs at all.

Desktop does not track you as the App Store defines tracking: nothing it sends is linked to you or your device, and nothing is shared with data brokers or advertisers. We do not sell or rent information about you, because we do not hold any.

What stays on your device

All of it, unless this policy says otherwise. Specifically:

  • Everything you save — links, images, videos — along with their titles, captions, creators and thumbnails.
  • Your folders, topics, ordering, wallpaper and appearance settings.
  • Whether you've finished onboarding, and your lifetime count of saved items (the number behind the free allowance).

Saved media is written to Desktop's own app container and to the App Group container it shares with its share extension. We run no backup or sync service, and we hold no copy of your library. The one exception is automatic topic sorting, which — only if you turn it on — sends individual saves for classification; that is described in full below.

What leaves your device

Four things, in specific moments. None of them carries an identifier for you or your device.

1. Fetching details about a link you saved

When you save a link, Desktop needs its title, creator and cover image to show you a real card instead of a bare URL. The link's address is sent to our enrich service, which requests the publicly available details for it. For ordinary web pages, your device fetches the page preview itself, so that request goes to the site directly and not through us.

Our service keeps no record of the link. Its operational logs record only the site's hostname (for example "youtube.com"), the provider used and whether the lookup succeeded — never the full address.

2. Sorting a save onto the right Desktop

Desktop can file each save under a topic for you. This is off until you turn it on. Desktop asks you once, the first time you open the Topics view, and does not send anything for sorting unless you agree. Agreeing applies from that point forward: saves already in your library are not sent, and stay in Unsorted until you file them yourself. You can change your mind at any time under Automatic Sorting in the app's menu; turning it off stops all further sending, and topics already assigned stay as they are. Turning it back on later sorts what you save from then on, not what accumulated while it was off. With it off, your saves still group by app and you can choose topics yourself.

When it is on, Desktop sends a short description of the item to our classify service, which passes it to a hosted AI model and gets back a single topic word. What gets sent depends on what you saved:

If you saved…Desktop sends…
A post or link Its title, caption, creator name and handle, community (such as a subreddit) and host, plus the post's cover thumbnail where the platform provides one.
One of your own photos or videos A reduced-size copy of the image, or up to two still frames taken from the video.

This is the one place where content you saved — including your own photos and videos — is transmitted off your device. It is used for nothing but choosing the topic word. We store none of it: the service holds it only for the moment it takes to answer, and its logs record only the source type, the model used and the outcome.

The request is passed to a hosted model provider (currently OpenAI; if that ever changes we will update this policy before it does). They do not use it to train their models. They may retain it briefly for their own abuse monitoring before deleting it. Because that window is longer than the request itself, we disclose this as data collection on the App Store rather than treat it as purely transient.

No account, user ID or device identifier accompanies the request, and we attach nothing that would let it be joined to anything else you saved. We want to be precise rather than reassuring about what that does and does not mean: it means we do not label the request with who you are. It does not mean the content itself is anonymous. A screenshot, caption or photo can contain a name, a face, an address or a private conversation, and stripping identifiers from the request does not remove any of that from the picture. That is the honest reason this is a choice you make rather than something Desktop does on your behalf.

3. Checking the app's configuration

Desktop asks our config service for current settings, such as the size of the free allowance. This request sends no information about you or your library.

4. Viewing something you saved

When you open a saved post or page, Desktop loads it from its original source — Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit, YouTube, or the website itself. Those requests go from your device to that platform, not through us. As with opening the same link in a browser, the platform receives your IP address and any cookies your device already holds for it, and what they do with that is governed by their privacy policies rather than this one. Desktop adds no identifier of its own to these requests and cannot see their contents.

Subscriptions and payment

Desktop's subscription is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the entire transaction. We never receive or have access to your payment method, card number or billing address. The app only asks iOS on your device whether a subscription is currently active; that answer never leaves your phone.

Your count of saved items is also kept in your device's keychain, so that reinstalling the app doesn't hand out a fresh set of free saves. It is stored only on that device, is never synchronised to iCloud or to us, and is a number — it contains nothing about what you saved.

Permissions Desktop does not ask for

Desktop requests no access to your photo library, camera, microphone, location, contacts or notifications, and does not ask permission to track. Items arrive through the iOS share sheet: you choose each one, and Desktop receives only what you shared with it.

Deleting your data

  • Delete any saved item inside the app. Deleting it removes the item and its media — including any reduced copy kept for sorting — from your device.
  • Deleting a folder removes only the grouping. The saves inside it stay in your library; delete them individually to remove their content.
  • Delete Desktop from your phone to remove its library, media and settings entirely.

Since we hold no library and no account, there is nothing for us to delete on your behalf and no account deletion request to make. The only value that deliberately outlives an uninstall is the lifetime save count described above.

If you'd like confirmation of any of this, or want to ask what we hold about you, write to us at the address below. The honest answer will normally be "nothing".

Children

Desktop is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13. Desktop can display the pages and posts you save, which come from the open web, so its App Store age rating reflects that its content is not filtered by us.

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your device, we will update the effective date above and describe the change here. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes rather than made quietly.
