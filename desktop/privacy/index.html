Privacy Policy

Desktop for iOS · Effective 8 November 2026

Desktop is a place to keep the things you save. Your library lives on your device. There is no account to create, and nothing you save is tied to your identity. If you turn on sync, which is optional and free, your library is encrypted on your device before it leaves, and the key is kept apart from the copy we store.

This policy explains exactly what stays on your phone, the few things that leave it and why, and who else is involved when you view something you saved. It describes the app as it actually works, not a category of app in general.

Desktop is published by VRKIVE LLC. "We" and "us" below mean VRKIVE LLC.

No account, and the two identifiers there are

Desktop has no sign-up, sign-in, profile or cloud library until you turn on sync, and most people never will. It does not read your device's advertising identifier or vendor identifier, and it has no crash reporting. Two services do run inside it: RevenueCat, which completes purchases and tells the app whether you subscribe, and PostHog, which counts how the app is used. Each gives your install a random identifier of its own. Those identifiers are tied to you only if you sign in for sync, and neither service ever receives what you saved. Both are described in their own sections below.

Desktop does not track you as the App Store defines tracking: nothing it collects is combined with data from other companies' apps or websites, and nothing is shared with data brokers or advertisers. We do not sell or rent information about you.

What stays on your device

All of it, unless this policy says otherwise. Specifically:

  • Everything you save — links, images, videos — along with their titles, captions, creators and thumbnails.
  • Your folders, topics, ordering, wallpaper and appearance settings.
  • Whether you've finished onboarding, and your lifetime count of saved items (the number behind the free allowance).

Saved media is written to Desktop's own app container and to the App Group container it shares with its share extension. Unless you turn on sync, we hold no copy of your library. Videos you save stay here in every case and are never sent anywhere, sync or not. The one exception to this section is automatic topic sorting, which sends details of a saved post or link for classification; that is described in full below.

What leaves your device

Four things about your library, in specific moments. None of them carries an identifier for you or your device. Purchases, usage analytics, sync, folder sharing and writing to us from the app each have a section of their own further down.

1. Fetching details about a link you saved

When you save a link, Desktop needs its title, creator and cover image to show you a real card instead of a bare URL. The link's address is sent to our enrich service, which requests the publicly available details for it. For ordinary web pages, your device fetches the page preview itself, so that request goes to the site directly and not through us.

Our service keeps no record of the link. Its operational logs record only the site's hostname (for example "youtube.com"), the provider used and whether the lookup succeeded — never the full address.

2. Sorting a save onto the right Desktop

Desktop files each save under a topic for you. It does this by sending a short description of what you saved to our classify service, which passes it to a hosted AI model and gets back a single topic word.

Your own photos and videos are never sent. Anything you save from your camera roll or from a file stays on your device and is never transmitted for sorting. Those saves stay in Unsorted until you choose a topic for them yourself, which you can do from the viewer at any time.

What is sent is drawn from the post or page you saved — material that was already published at the address you saved it from:

If you saved…Desktop sends…
A post or link Its title, caption, creator name and handle, community (such as a subreddit) and host, plus the post's cover thumbnail where the platform provides one.
One of your own photos or videos Nothing. These are never sent.

It is used for nothing but choosing the topic word. We store none of it: the service holds it only for the moment it takes to answer, and its logs record only the source type, the model used and the outcome.

The request is passed to a hosted model provider (currently OpenAI; if that ever changes we will update this policy before it does). We have turned off retention on our side of that account, and they do not use it to train their models. They may retain it briefly for their own abuse monitoring before deleting it. Because that window is longer than the request itself, we disclose this as data collection on the App Store rather than treat it as purely transient.

No account, user ID or device identifier accompanies the request, and we attach nothing that would let it be joined to anything else you saved.

We would rather be precise than reassuring about what that does and does not mean. It means we do not label the request with who you are. It does not mean the content itself is anonymous: a title or caption can carry a name, and the page you saved may say something about you simply by being the page you saved. What it does mean is that the material in question was already public at the address you saved it from before Desktop ever touched it. That distinction is why your own photos and videos are excluded from this entirely rather than merely disclosed — a picture out of your library was never anywhere, and no wording in a policy would make sending it the same kind of act.

Sorting is not retroactive: a library that accumulated before this feature existed is not sent, and those saves stay in Unsorted until you file them.

You can set a topic yourself on any save, from the ⋯ menu in the viewer, and a topic you choose is permanent — automatic sorting never overrides it. Your saves also group by app independently of any of this, under Apps.

3. Checking the app's configuration

Desktop asks our config service for current settings, such as the size of the free allowance. This request sends no information about you or your library.

4. Viewing something you saved

When you open a saved post or page, Desktop loads it from its original source — Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit, YouTube, or the website itself. Those requests go from your device to that platform, not through us. As with opening the same link in a browser, the platform receives your IP address and any cookies your device already holds for it, and what they do with that is governed by their privacy policies rather than this one. Desktop adds no identifier of its own to these requests and cannot see their contents.

Sync (optional, encrypted)

Sync keeps your library the same on every device you use. It is off until you open the Sync screen in the menu and sign in with Apple, and it is free. Until you do that, nothing in this section applies to you.

Encrypted on your device. Before anything leaves your phone, Desktop encrypts it: your saved links with their titles, captions, creators and topics; your screenshots and photos saved from your own library; the frame used as a video's cover; your folders, their names and which saves are in them. We store that encrypted copy. Videos themselves are never uploaded and stay on the device that saved them.

The key. The key is not stored with your library. Our sync service derives it for your account from a secret kept outside the database, and sends it to your device each time it syncs, so there is no key to carry between devices and none to lose. A copy of the database on its own, from a breach or a leaked backup, cannot be read.

What we can see. Because our service holds the secret the key comes from, it can open your library, and so could we. Syncing itself never does: it moves encrypted records without opening them. Without the key, the service sees an identifier for your account and for each record, whether a record is a save, a folder or a folder membership, when records change or are deleted, how large the encrypted files are, and when your devices sync.

Signing in. Desktop asks Apple for a sign-in only, not your name or email address. Apple gives our sign-in service a stable identifier for you, unique to Desktop, which we use only to tell your encrypted library apart from everyone else's.

Your subscription, if you have one. Sync does not depend on it. Once you sign in, RevenueCat (see Purchases and subscriptions) tells our service when your subscription starts, renews or ends, and we keep the product and its expiry against your account. Nothing about payment comes with it.

Where it lives. On Supabase, in the United States, under an account we hold with them. It is reachable only through your sign-in.

Deleting it. Tap Delete Account on the Sync screen and everything synced is removed at once, together with the RevenueCat customer record and the PostHog usage history that carry your account identifier. Sign Out stops sync but leaves what is already there. A sync account that has not synced for a year may be deleted. Deleting the app alone does not delete the synced copy; use Delete Account first.

Sharing a folder

You can share any folder as a link, whether or not you subscribe. Nothing is shared until you choose Share Folder and send the link yourself.

What is in it. The folder's name and emoji, and for each saved link: its address, title, caption, creator, the details Desktop shows on its card, and a small cover image. Photos, screenshots and videos from your own library are never included.

Encrypted on your device. The folder is encrypted on your phone with a new key for each link. The encrypted copy is uploaded to our share service; the key travels only inside the link, in the part after the #, which browsers and apps never send to a server. We store the encrypted copy and cannot read it. Anyone you give the link to, and anyone they pass it on to, can open the folder.

No account. Uploading a folder carries no account or device identifier. To limit abuse, the service counts uploads per network address for an hour, using a scrambled form of the address that changes every hour and is deleted after two.

Opening a link. In Desktop, the folder is downloaded and decrypted on the recipient's device. On the web, the page at desktopsaves.com decrypts it in the browser; that page is hosted by GitHub, which receives the visitor's network address as for any website.

How long it lasts. A link works for 30 days. After that our service stops serving it and deletes the encrypted copy at its next cleanup. Anyone who added the folder to their own Desktop keeps their copy.

Purchases and subscriptions

Desktop's subscription is sold through the App Store, and Apple handles the entire transaction. We never receive or have access to your payment method, card number or billing address.

RevenueCat. Purchases are completed through RevenueCat, a subscription service we use so the app can tell whether you subscribe, including renewals, cancellations, refunds and offer-code redemptions that happen while the app is closed. RevenueCat receives the App Store's signed record of your purchases, the app version and the kind of device, and a random customer identifier it assigns to your install. It is also told the random identifier PostHog assigns (below), so the two services describe the same install, and it forwards subscription events such as a trial starting or a renewal to PostHog under that identifier. It never sees what you saved. If you sign in for sync, your sync account identifier takes the place of the random one, so a purchase follows your account to your other devices. RevenueCat is in the United States and processes this under its own privacy policy and our agreement with it.

Your count of saved items is also kept in your device's keychain, so that reinstalling the app doesn't hand out a fresh set of free saves. It is stored only on that device, is never synchronised to iCloud or to us, and is a number — it contains nothing about what you saved.

Usage analytics

Desktop sends a short record of how it is used to PostHog, an analytics service, so we can tell whether the app earns its place: whether people keep saving, what they do with folders, and what happens at the paywall. This is on for everyone; there is no switch for it in the app, and we would rather say so than imply one.

What is sent. A named event at about ten moments: a save (with the app it came from, such as Instagram, and how many items), opening a saved item, creating a folder, sharing or receiving one, changing a sort order, opening search, seeing the paywall and how you left it, finishing onboarding, and sending us feedback. PostHog's library adds that the app was opened, installed or updated. Every event carries the app version, the size of the free allowance, whether you subscribe, and the device model, iOS version, language and time zone that PostHog's library attaches to all events, along with the network address it arrives from, which PostHog uses to estimate a region. No event ever carries a link, a title, a caption, a filename, a folder name, your message to us, or anything else you saved.

Session replays. For one session in five, PostHog records a replay: a sequence of screenshots of the app as you use it. Text you type and images are masked by PostHog's library, and the feed of your saves is masked by Desktop everywhere it appears, so what remains is the shape of the screens you moved through. We use it to see where onboarding and the paywall lose people.

Who you are to it. Until you sign in for sync, PostHog knows your install by a random identifier. If you sign in, that identifier is tied to your sync account, so what you did before and after signing in is one history, and Delete Account on the Sync screen removes it. Deleting the app discards the identifier, and a reinstall starts a new one.

Where. PostHog's servers in the United States, under an account we hold with them.

Writing to us from the app

The menu's Feedback & Support row opens a sheet for a problem, an idea or a question. Sending it posts the message to our feedback service, which stores it and emails it to support@arkive.tech. With your words go the facts the sheet lists on screen before you send: the app version and build, your device model, iOS version and language, whether you are on Premium or the free plan and that month's allowance, and whether sync is on. A reply address goes only if you type one; without it we cannot answer, but the message still reaches us. Nothing about your library, your sync account or your analytics identifier goes with it.

To limit abuse, the service counts messages per network address for an hour, using a scrambled form of the address that changes every hour and is deleted after two, as folder sharing does. The email is delivered through Resend, an email service in the United States, which handles the message in order to deliver it. We keep messages until they have been dealt with; write to the address below if you want one deleted sooner.

Permissions Desktop does not ask for

Desktop requests no access to your photo library, camera, microphone, location, contacts or notifications, and does not ask permission to track. Items arrive through the iOS share sheet: you choose each one, and Desktop receives only what you shared with it.

Deleting your data

  • Delete any saved item inside the app. Deleting it removes the item and its media — including any reduced copy kept for sorting — from your device.
  • Deleting a folder removes only the grouping. The saves inside it stay in your library; delete them individually to remove their content.
  • Delete Desktop from your phone to remove its library, media and settings entirely.

If you never turned on sync, we hold no library and no account. What exists about your install is held under random identifiers: a usage history at PostHog and a purchase record at RevenueCat. We cannot connect either to you from the outside, which also means we cannot find them on request; deleting the app discards both identifiers, and a reinstall starts afresh. If you did turn on sync, Delete Account on the Sync screen removes everything synced immediately, along with the RevenueCat record and PostHog history under your account identifier; no request to us is needed. A shared folder's encrypted copy is deleted after its link expires at 30 days. Feedback you sent us is kept until it has been dealt with. The only value that deliberately outlives an uninstall is the lifetime save count described above.

If you'd like confirmation of any of this, or want to ask what we hold about you, write to us at the address below.

Children

Desktop is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13. Desktop can display the pages and posts you save, which come from the open web, so its App Store age rating reflects that its content is not filtered by us.

Changes

8 November 2026: three services now run inside the app where before there were none. Purchases are completed through RevenueCat, which receives the App Store's record of your purchases under a random identifier, and the app no longer sends Apple's signed transaction to our own service. Usage is counted by PostHog, which receives about ten named events and a sampled, masked session replay, never anything you saved; this replaces the statement that the app contained no analytics. The menu's Support row became Feedback & Support, which sends your message and the build facts it shows you to our feedback service instead of opening your mail app. Delete Account now also removes the RevenueCat record and PostHog history tied to your account. The sections on purchases, usage analytics and writing to us describe each.

29 October 2026: sync is now free for every account, and its encryption changed. Until now the key was made on your device and never sent to us, so we could not read a synced library. Now our sync service derives the key for your account and holds the secret it comes from, so a copy of the database alone still cannot be read, but we could open your library if we chose to. The section on sync above describes the new arrangement. Nothing changed for a library that has never been synced.

13 October 2026: added sync, an optional Premium feature that is off until you sign in with Apple on the Sync screen, and folder sharing. Both are encrypted on your device before anything is uploaded; the new sections describe what is stored, what we can and cannot see, and how it is deleted. For anyone who uses neither, nothing about what leaves the device has changed.

29 September 2026: automatic topic sorting no longer sends photos or videos from your own library. Only saved posts and links are sent for sorting, and only the details that were already public at the address you saved them from; camera-roll media now stays on your device in every case. The model provider is named directly, we have turned off retention on our side of that account, and we have set out that sorting is not retroactive. Clarified that a saved item's content can itself contain personal information even though we attach no identifier to it, and that deleting a folder does not delete the saves inside it.

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your device, we will update the effective date above and describe the change here. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes rather than made quietly.
